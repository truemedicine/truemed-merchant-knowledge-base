title: "New Year, New You: The Budget Holiday Spending Didn't Touch"
---

January's problem is not intent, it's budget. Your customers genuinely want to get healthier, and they just spent December's paycheck on everyone else. Conversion lags intent all month.

A new benefit year starts January 1: FSA balances reset to the new election, and HSA contributions renew for the year. That money can only go toward health, which makes it the one budget your customer has that holiday spending did not touch. Resolution messaging is the most crowded category in January, so the brands that win are the ones pointing at money that already exists.

**What you're launching:** two emails, three ads, one feed post, one story slide, and one copy swap on your abandoned-cart flow. Every word is written below. Plan on an afternoon.

<Note>
**The message:** A new benefit year means new health funds. Start the year with them.

Say "save an average of 30% for qualified customers." Never say "covered," "free," or "everything qualifies." Do not tell customers their HSA balance "reset": HSA funds roll over and accumulate, only FSA balances reset. "A new benefit year" is accurate for both. Every template below is pre-checked.
</Note>

## Season-long settings

## Your ads

Live by January 2.

Q4 pulled every advertiser's budget forward, so January reach is cheap while health intent sits at its annual peak. The angle writes itself: you are not asking anyone to find room in a budget that holiday spending already emptied. You are pointing at money set aside for health that can only be spent on health.

Resolution angles win the subject line in January, so lead with the goal and put the new-funds hook in the preview text. Both templates below include one.
